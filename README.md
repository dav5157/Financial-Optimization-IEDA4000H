IEDA 4000H: Optimal Rebalancing Research
Project Report

HUNG Tin Ching, HUNG Wing Sum Angela, LAM Ngai Man, and NG David Ian, Group 1

Department of Industrial Engineering and Decision Analytics, HKUST, Hong Kong SAR.
The order is arranged based on the alphabetical order. These authors contributed equally to this
work.


Abstract
This research project investigates the development of optimal portfolio rebalancing
strategies, focusing on the critical trade-off between maintaining target allocations
and minimizing transaction costs. Through a combination of theoretical derivation,
comprehensive simulations, and sensitivity analysis, we evaluate the performance of
periodic and threshold-based rebalancing mechanisms under varying market condi-
tions and cost structures. Our findings demonstrate that, under typical institutional
cost assumptions, a theoretically optimized periodic schedule (e.g., monthly) provides
superior economic efficiency and net returns compared to a dynamic threshold strat-
egy, which incurs excessive trading costs despite tighter allocation control. The analy-
sis further reveals the regime-dependent nature of rebalancing efficacy and highlights
the importance of accurately modeling cost structures (proportional vs. fixed). We
conclude that while threshold rebalancing offers precise allocation management, its
practical value is often eroded by costs, making periodic rebalancing a more robust
and implementable baseline for most institutional investors.
